Operator Algebra

MOAD.Algebra is the symbolic second-quantized layer: it defines the sites and their modes, the ladder operators, and the OperatorSum algebra in which every MOAD Hamiltonian and observable is written. Expressions are built to read like the physics — c'(s, 1) * c(s, 2) is the operator $\hat{c}^\dagger_{s\uparrow}\,\hat{c}_{s\downarrow}$ — with fermionic anticommutation tracked exactly and symbolically; nothing is materialized as a matrix until the Hilbert Spaces & Bases layer.

Sites and modes

A site is a local Hilbert space with a fixed set of modes. Three kinds: FermionSite{N} (N fermionic modes), BosonSite (a truncated boson), and SpinSite (a spin-S). Sites are named; modes are addressed by 1-based index.

using MOAD

s = FermionSite{2}(:imp)        # a 2-mode fermion site (e.g. ↑, ↓)
(local_dim(s), statistics(s))
(4, Fermionic())

Ladder operators

The fermionic ladder operators are c / cdag (also written c'), the bosonic ones b / bdag, the number operator n (and n_b), and the spin operators Sx/Sy/Sz/Splus/Sminus. A single ladder operator and any sum/product of them is an OperatorSum; arithmetic (+, -, *, scalar *) and the adjoint ' are defined.

n_up = cdag(s, 1) * c(s, 1)     # number operator for mode 1
(n_up == n(s, 1), (cdag(s, 1) * c(s, 2))' isa OperatorSum)
(true, true)

Fermionic anticommutation is enforced symbolically: reordering ladder operators carries the Jordan–Wigner sign automatically, so the algebra is exact regardless of the order in which terms are written.

OperatorSum utilities

chop drops terms below a tolerance (cleaning up numerical roundoff after arithmetic), and add_hc adds the Hermitian conjugate. rotate applies a single-particle basis rotation. Operators serialize with save_operator / load_operator.

H = -1.0 * (cdag(s, 1) * c(s, 2))
H = add_hc(H)                   # H + H†  (Hermitian hopping)
length(H)
2

Conserved quantities

Particle-number and spin observables — n_fermion, n_boson, Sz_total, and the general WeightedParticleCount — are QuantumNumber objects. Compared against a value with == (or ∈ a range) they produce a Restriction used to carve out a conserved sector; this is the subject of the Hilbert Spaces & Bases chapter.
